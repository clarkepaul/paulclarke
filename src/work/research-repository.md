## Context

By 2022 Totara's research space held more than 500 pages of partner meetings, customer calls and interview notes going back to 2017. They were filed by region and year, written in different formats, and named inconsistently. To find out who had already spoken to customers about reporting, or what partners had said about a feature, you had to know which folder to open.

## My role

As Head of Design, I own the research directory and lead research operations. I restructured the space in October 2022 with two researchers, and have maintained it since as product managers across R&D took over most of the contributions.

## The problem

Research existed, but nobody could find it. The result was repeated conversations, insights that stayed with the person who gathered them, and roadmap discussions that relied on memory rather than evidence.

## What I did

- **A directory with a shared record format.** One template for every research note, and a simple path for adding older notes. Each record carries the same properties, which roll up into a single searchable table.
- **Guidance for people who are not researchers.** A research introduction and getting-started template, methods and activities, research operations with a participant database, and a backlog of discovery questions.
- **Synthesis people can reuse.** Partner and customer profiles, a jobs-to-be-done database, user archetypes and personas, and competitor profiles.
- **A route into decisions.** The evidence links to the [product health model](/case-study-product-health-model.html), so research informs how each product area is scored, and from there what gets confirmed on the roadmap.

## Key decisions

- **Keep the record small.** Each record has six fields that a product manager can complete within a minute of finishing a call: date, keywords, owner, participants, region and a summary. The fields roll up into a live table at the top of the directory, which people filter and search without opening individual pages.
- **Design for contributors, not researchers.** A research team of two could never record every conversation. The guidance and templates are written so that a product manager can run and record discovery without help.
- **Put the directory in the middle.** Conversations, studies and surveys go in; shared synthesis, product health scoring and roadmap decisions come out. It is the one place both people and AI tools read from when evidence is needed.

## Outcome

- **It became other people's tool.** 408 entries have been added since the restructure, 97% of them by colleagues rather than by me, from 16 contributors across product and R&D.
- **Steady contribution.** New entries per year: 60 in the last quarter of 2022, 82 in 2023, 116 in 2024, 71 in 2025 and 79 in 2026 to early November. Contribution held through team changes, which suggests the habit belongs to the product teams rather than to a research function.
- **More than 1,000 pages are now findable,** including notes back to 2017.
- **Evidence behind product health.** Directory research gives each product area's health score a traceable source, and roadmap decisions are checked against what customers and partners have said rather than what people remember.
- **Ready for AI.** Because the records are consistent, AI tools now read the directory to give leadership summarised views of customer evidence.

## Reflection

Consistent, minimal metadata did most of the work. It made the directory easy to contribute to, and it is what later made the records readable by AI. Next, I would:

- map free-text keywords to the capability areas in the product health model, so roll-ups by area are exact rather than approximate
- add a light "key finding" field, because most records are call notes, and a finding makes synthesis faster for people and for AI
- link records to feature requests and to shipped work, to show which evidence changed a decision
- set a clear retention rule for participant details, so the archive stays useful and compliant as it grows
