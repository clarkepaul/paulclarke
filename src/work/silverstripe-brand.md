## Context

Silverstripe is a New Zealand company best known for Silverstripe CMS, an open-source content management system. Over time it grew a set of products and services around the CMS, including Silverstripe Cloud, its platform for site management, hosting and a deployment pipeline.

For years everything sat under one company brand. As the products matured, each one needed to be recognisable on its own while still clearly belonging to Silverstripe.

## My role

I worked on the Silverstripe brand across most of its life, and led design for its product brands alongside my role leading the experience and design of Silverstripe CMS and Silverstripe Cloud. On the company brand, I worked on the second version under the direction of Silverstripe's creative director at the time, designed the next version myself, and created the current version with another designer at Silverstripe. I led the product brands, and two designers in my team contributed to the Silverstripe Cloud brand, which I used as a chance for them to learn brand design.

## The problem

- **One brand doing every job.** The company, the open-source CMS and the commercial hosting platform leaned on the same brand, so it was hard to tell a product from the company behind it. Separating them felt risky: there was a fear that the CMS would lose traction without its well-known name.
- **A brand that had aged.** Each earlier version had its own weaknesses, from the colours and type of the original to a mark that was too big and too subdued to reuse well.
- **New products needed a place.** Silverstripe Cloud, and later a platform for public sector customers, needed identities that fitted the family rather than one-off logos. Each one arrived at a different time, so the family had to grow one brand at a time.

## Evolving the company brand

The Silverstripe mark has always been an S drawn from two linked strokes. Each version kept that idea and fixed what the one before had got wrong:

- **The original** had problems with its colouring, kerning and choice of typeface, and an awkward tension where the strokes overlap.
- **The second version** had an oversized mark, and its colours were so subdued that they were hard to reuse across different media.
- **The current version**, which I created with another designer at Silverstripe, has a more modern typeface and a bright colour that makes it punchier and easier to use everywhere.

## One glyph, a family of marks

Rather than draw a new logo for each product, I built every mark from the same glyph: a single rounded stroke fitted to a grid, outlined, combined into a shape and rotated. The S for the company, a cloud for Silverstripe Cloud and a linked loop for Silverstripe CMS all share the same curves, weight and colour, so they read as one family.

## Silverstripe Cloud

Silverstripe Cloud brings site management, hosting and the deployment pipeline together. Its mark draws a cloud from the same linked stroke, and I carried the brand through the product: the platform's deployments, CMS access, metrics and configuration screens, the new service desk migrated from Freshdesk, and the documentation and emails customers receive. I checked that the interface stayed accessible, and stayed involved from design through testing to the go-live with the operations team.

## Silverstripe Cloud Public Sector

The New Zealand Government Common Web Platform was an all-of-government service, with a procurement process that let agencies fast-track getting a website. When it closed, Silverstripe launched Silverstripe Cloud Public Sector in November 2021 to take its place.

Underneath, it was the same platform as Silverstripe Cloud and the Common Web Platform, with two differences for government: extra approval steps in the deployment pipeline, to protect security and keep data within New Zealand, and plugins that organisations could share with each other.

I designed its brand: a mark built on the same grid from rounded shapes, with lockups and spacing rules so the team could apply it consistently. Nearly all Common Web Platform customers moved to Silverstripe Cloud Public Sector, and a couple chose Silverstripe Cloud instead.

## Silverstripe CMS

The last step was to give the CMS a brand of its own, independent of the company, replacing the company S with the CMS's own linked-loop mark. I prepared the launch, and the new brand went live as I left Silverstripe to join Totara at the start of 2022.

## Outcome

- **A family customers could read.** The company and each product had their own mark, and all of them clearly belonged to Silverstripe.
- **Room to grow.** Silverstripe Cloud Public Sector got a brand that fitted the family when it launched, without starting from scratch, and nearly all Common Web Platform customers moved across to it.
- **A CMS that stands on its own.** Silverstripe CMS launched with its own brand, separate from the company. Rather than losing traction, the change made both the CMS and the business clearer about what each one offers, and improved the messaging for both.
- **The brand in the product, not only on the website.** Customers met the same brand in the platform, the service desk, the documentation and their emails.

## Reflection

- **Design the family as a unit.** The brands were added one at a time as each product arrived. That organic approach had its challenges, and it would have been easier to design the whole family together, even if some brands launched later.
- **Separate company and product much earlier.** The fear that the CMS would lose traction without the company name held the change back for too long. In the end, separating them made both the CMS and the business stronger.
